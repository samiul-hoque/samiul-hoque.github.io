## Context

Nazm Anwr is an architect, a calligrapher, and the flute player for the
Bangladeshi band Kaaktaal. We have known each other since university and we
do each other favours. All sorts, in both directions, and nobody is keeping
score.

He owns more flutes than he performs with. Thirteen of them go out with him
for a show, and they are not one instrument in different sizes. The largest
is about three feet long and the smallest is a few inches. Some are bamboo,
bound with thread and tape. The rest are metal or black resin: aluminium,
copper, brass. Among them are a Tony Dixon DX207 in A and another in G, a
Tony Dixon TB014 tenor flute, a Tony Dixon DX001 soprano whistle, a brass
Generation whistle in Eb and a tin Clarke whistle in C. During a set he
swaps between them, and he does not stand still while he plays. He walks
the stage.

## A saxophone mic on a flute

Traditional flutes have no good microphone options. There is no pickup
built in, nothing to clip to, and no standard mounting point anywhere on
the body. A stand microphone would solve the audio and ruin the
performance, because he would have to stay in front of it.

He had already bought the hardware: a NUX B-6 Pro, the wireless mic system
sold for saxophone. The B-6 clamps onto the bell of a sax, which is a rigid
flared metal mouth of one known size. A flute is a straight tube, and he
owns more than a dozen across a range of diameters and several materials.
The mic had no way to attach to any of them.

So the job had two halves that pull against each other. Whatever holds the
mic has to grip hard enough that it will not rotate or creep during a song,
on a surface that is smooth and slightly irregular. It also has to come off
one flute and go onto the next in the gap between songs, by hand, in stage
light, without tools.

## What we made

Nazm is a maker himself, so this was a joint design session rather than a
brief handed over. What we settled on was a clamp split into two layers
that do two different jobs.

The outer layer is rigid, printed in PETG. It is a split ring with a boss
on one side that reproduces the mount the B-6 expects to find on a
saxophone, closed by a printed thumb screw that threads straight into the
ring.

The inner layer is flexible, printed in TPU. It takes up the difference
between the clamp bore and the actual flute, and it grips without marking
the instrument. A rigid clamp tightened straight onto a bamboo flute would
either slip or dent it, and onto a thin aluminium tube it would crush it.
The TPU does the conforming so the PETG does not have to.

Splitting the two layers is what made the set tractable in a day. Instead
of modelling thirteen bespoke clamps, we made five clamp sizes and a set of
TPU spacers that step each clamp down to whichever flutes fall under it.

| Clamp bore | Spacer inner diameters |
| --- | --- |
| 22 mm | 15, 16, 20 |
| 24 mm | 22, 22.5 |
| 28 mm | 24.5, 26.5 |
| 30 mm | 27.5 |
| 35 mm | 30, 33 |

Five clamps, ten spacers. Everything printed, including the screws.
Printed fasteners were not a purity exercise: it meant no hardware
run on the day, nothing metal to lose in a green room, and a thumb screw
big enough to turn by hand, which is the part that matters when the swap
has to happen between songs.

## Measure, fit, number

The part of the day that actually took the time was not the modelling. It
was going through the collection one flute at a time with a pair of
callipers, finding which clamp and which spacer it wanted, and writing the
pairing down before it got lost.

We numbered the flutes and photographed each one as it was fitted, with the
clamp size over the spacer size scrawled on the picture in red. The three
largest bamboo flutes all take 35 over 33. The thin brass and grey metal
ones come down to 22 over 15 and 22 over 16. A few are annotated with the
measured diameter instead, 26.16 or 27.5, where the reading mattered more
than the pair.

Several flutes turned out close enough in diameter to share a pairing,
which is the reason five clamps and ten spacers cover thirteen
instruments rather than needing one of each.

It is a crude system and it worked. The photo roll became the mapping
document, which is worth more than it sounds: without it the set is fifteen
anonymous black rings and a pile of flutes, and matching them up again
means redoing the measuring.

## One day

The deadline was a Kaaktaal show at Aloki on 4 September, and the whole
job got one day. That timeline decides the method more than anything else.
Every flute had to be measured before any modelling started, the clamp had
to be one parametric model with the bore driven by a parameter rather than
five separate files, and the prints had to be batched and running while the
next size was still being drawn.

## Outcome

The clamps held up through the show, and he is still using them. That is
the whole test. A clamp that grips fine on the bench and works loose
halfway through a set would have been a failure, and one day was not
enough time to prove in advance that it would not happen.

## If you have a B-6 and a flute

This is a common enough problem that it is worth spelling out what these
parts are. A NUX B-6 or B-6 Pro expects a saxophone bell. If you play any
transverse flute, a bamboo one, a metal one, a whistle, there is nothing on
the instrument for it to hold. These clamps sit between the two: a printed
split ring that carries the standard B-6 clip, and a soft TPU liner sized
to your tube.

The bores here are 22, 24, 28, 30 and 35 mm with spacers down to 15 mm,
which covers a good part of the usual range. If your flute falls between
sizes the fix is a spacer at your diameter, not a new clamp, and that is a
five-minute change to one parameter.

### Instruments in the set

Most of Nazm's flutes are handmade bamboo, which have no model number and no
two alike. The shop-bought ones do, so if you play one of these, these
clamps have been fitted to that exact instrument:

- **Tony Dixon DX207**, key of A — aluminium
- **Tony Dixon DX207**, key of G — aluminium
- **Tony Dixon TB014** tenor flute — polymer
- **Tony Dixon DX001** soprano whistle — polymer
- **Generation** whistle, key of Eb — brass
- **Clarke** whistle, key of C — tin

Measure yours anyway before you print. We recorded the clamp and spacer
pairing per flute on the photographs, not per model, so I cannot tell you
which pair a DX207 takes without going back to the pictures — and Tony
Dixon have changed tube stock between production runs before. Callipers
across the point where you want the mic, then pick from the table above.
